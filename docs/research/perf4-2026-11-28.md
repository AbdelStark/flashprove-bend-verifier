# Sixteen workers against one-thread C++, 2026-11-28

> Status: frozen

REQ-PERF-4 asks that, with 16 Bend workers, the median full-size exact-match call take no longer than single-thread C++ on the same host. The [matched runs](matched-2026-11-27.md) left Bend 1.12× (Xeon) and 1.19× (M4 Max) behind `cp_verify -j 1`. This build closes that gap on both hosts. [Performance contract](../spec/performance.md)

The build is `5fe8190`: main at `fd3a144` plus four changes. Its Bend source digest is `71a95dc97b7819dca6bcf8badfc47cfa9335ee8a20d32685e2ebdddb28894f1c`.

- The Xeon is a Platinum 8480CL in Slurm job 7822, with 16 dedicated physical cores, under Linux 6.8 with Ubuntu clang 18.1.3. Each pairing is pinned to one logical CPU per core, either 1 core or 16, as in the matched runs. One other job held 2 CPUs on the node.
- The M4 Max is under macOS 26.6.2 with Apple Clang 21.0.0, unpinned.

Every call ran in a fresh process after one verified warmup per program and matched the four published closing lines. [Raw results](data/perf4/)

## Where the time went

Timestamps around the request's phases, on main with 16 workers on the M4, split the 9.4 s call as follows. Only the segment chain gained from workers:

| Phase | 1 worker | 16 workers |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Public, key and leaf stages | 1.9 s | 2.0 s |
| First chain's transcript walk | 1.6 s | 1.6 s |
| Segment chain | 11.6 s | 4.5 s |
| Binding chains | 1.2 s | 1.2 s |

Inside the segment chain, the 25 local equations take 7.3 s and already run beside the ordered transport. The transport itself took 4.0 s at one worker, and the 16-worker chain took the same 4.0 s: it was bound by transport. Half of each transport step was projecting the folded columns. The queue that was meant to run two projections beside the next transition ran them in a fork nested inside the pipeline's own fork, and the runtime deals forks to idle cores only while a fork tree grows. Those projections ran on one core.

## What changed

1. **Projections wait until the chain ends.** The next link reads the preceding mask, target and kept rows, but never the projected columns. Each update now waits, and the chain ends by computing all contributions as one balanced fork tree at the top level, then applying `C_i = contribution_i + carry_i · C_(i−1)` in transcript order. The tree splits the updates into unshared parts first: sharing the list of updates, which hold whole forms, cost 0.7 s by itself. One worker keeps projecting each update at once. [Transport](../../src/segment_transport.bend) · [Equivalence test over five updates](../../tests/segment_transport.bend)
2. **The hash reads its input four bytes at a time.** `update` used to count the input before compressing it, walking a shared list. It now compresses each full block once a byte follows it, recursing on the input itself. [Hash](../../src/hash_bytes.bend) · [Comparison with the old reader at every length to 299](../../tests/hash_bytes.bend)
3. **Merkle vertices and sessions have fixed-width keys.** A vertex was keyed by its decimal depth and place, a session by the root's hex. Both are now short strings of words. [Keys](../../src/opening.bend) · [Distinct across 2³²](../../tests/opening.bend)
4. **Columns and the reader's buffer are cut in one pass.** Each was taken and then dropped from a shared list. [Openings](../../src/opening_bytes.bend) · [Reader](../../src/wire_reader.bend)

Three experiments made the call slower and were dropped: running the binding chains beside the segment chain (a pure parallel branch serialized the chain's own forks, 14.7 s), windows of 4 or 16 rows instead of 8 (8.1 and 8.3 s), and computing the carried and symbol rows of a transition side by side (8.4 s).

## Conformance

On `5fe8190` on the M4: `make ci`, `make standalone-full`, `make stages-full`, `make standalone-extended`, `make worker-parity` and `make fuzz FUZZ_ARGS="-n 64 --seed 7"` passed. Every benchmark call above returned the expected closing lines.

## Limits

- The Xeon figures are medians of three calls, as in the matched runs; the M4 figures of seven and five.
- `cp_verify -j 1` sets only the request stage's threads. On 16 cores its other stages use more. The Xeon's one-core pairing is the only strictly single-thread C++ figure.
- The remaining serial work is the public, key and leaf stages, the transcript walks and the transport transitions, most of the 7.3 s on the M4.
